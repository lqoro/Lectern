## 1. You are responsible for what you record

Laws on recording conversations differ by country and by state. In some
places — for example several US states, including California, Florida,
Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and
Washington — **everyone being recorded may have to consent**. Other places
require one party's consent, and some treat classrooms differently.

Schools and instructors also set their own rules. Many **do not allow lectures
to be recorded without permission**, unless recording is an approved
accommodation (for example through your school's disability services office).
Course recordings and slides may be protected by copyright, and some schools
forbid sharing them.

Before you record or import a lecture:

- check your school's policy and your instructor's rules, and ask permission
  when in doubt;
- follow the recording laws where you are;
- use recordings for your own study, and don't share or publish them without
  permission.

Lectern does not check any of this for you. This notice is general
information, **not legal advice**.

## 2. Transcripts and notes can be wrong

Speech recognition and AI-written notes make mistakes: misheard words, missed
sentences, wrong terms or numbers. Always check important details against the
recording or your course materials. Do not rely on Lectern alone for medical,
clinical, legal, safety or exam-critical information.
